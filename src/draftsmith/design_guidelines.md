# What a good residential plan looks like

Design doctrine for drafting agents. Syntax and protocol are in the
main prompt; this is judgment. Sources and the scoring rules that
mirror these guidelines: docs/evaluation.md (NBC India / IRC codes,
Hillier & Hanson space syntax, Alexander's patterns).

## Think program first, walls second

Before drawing anything, commit to a program: the room list with
target areas, and which rooms connect. Group rooms into three zones —
**public** (living, dining), **service** (kitchen, utility), and
**private** (bedrooms, baths) — and plan the plan so a visitor moves
public → service → private, each step deeper from the entrance. A
guest should reach the living room and a toilet without brushing past
bedrooms; bedrooms should sit at the quiet end of the circulation.

## Circulation

- Every room reachable from the entrance through doors — obvious, and
  still the most common failure. Trace the path room by room.
- At least one bath/WC reachable without passing through any bedroom.
  Never make one bedroom the only way into another.
- Corridors ≥ 900 mm wide, and stingy: 5–15% of floor area is spent
  well; more is waste, none at all usually means rooms have become
  corridors. No dead-end corridor legs serving nothing.
- A circulation ring (two ways around, e.g. kitchen–dining–living
  loop) makes a house feel generous; a pure access tree is acceptable
  but stiff. Rings through bedrooms are not generous, just leaky.
- One door per room pair. Total doors ≈ number of rooms (± a few).
  Extra doors between the same two rooms add noise, not access.

## Rooms: size and shape

Minimums (NBC India; do not go below — and don't hover at them either):

| room | min area | min width |
|---|---|---|
| living / first habitable | 9.5 m² | 2.4 m |
| bedroom | 7.5 m² (comfortable: 12+) | 2.1 m |
| kitchen | 5.0 m² | 1.8 m |
| bath | 1.8 m² | 1.1 m |
| WC | 1.1 m² | 0.9 m |

Keep habitable rooms between 1:1 and 1:1.5 in aspect; past 1:2 a room
reads as a corridor. Rooms don't have to be rectangles — an L-shaped
living room that carves out a dining zone is better than a wall — but
every part of a room must be wide enough to use.

## Doors and windows

- Widths: entrance and habitable-room doors ≥ 900 mm; bath/WC ≥ 750 mm.
- Keep opening edges ≥ 100 mm clear of wall junctions; a door jammed
  into a corner can't be framed or trimmed.
- Swing doors into the room they serve, leaf against the nearest wall.
- Windows only in exterior walls. Every habitable room needs one, with
  glass area near 1/10 of the floor area. Rooms lit from **two
  directions** feel dramatically better than one — corner rooms are an
  opportunity, use them.
- Kitchens and baths want a window or ventilator too. A bath/WC door
  must never open directly into the kitchen.

## Use the vocabulary

Style variety is part of design quality, applied with intent — not
sprinkled: sliding doors for balconies and tight spots; double doors
where living opens wide to dining or passage; window styles varied by
room importance. A plan that uses one style for everything reads as a
first draft.

## Drafting discipline

- One label inside every room; put it where it won't collide with a
  neighbouring label in a narrow room.
- Dimensions live OUTSIDE the building — overall width and height at
  minimum. Check the offset sign puts the line outside, not slicing
  through rooms.

## Character

The failure mode to avoid is the boring grid: equal rectangles in
rows, every door arc-style, every window identical. Good plans have a
legible idea — an articulated footprint (L/T/U beats a featureless
box), zones distinguished by geometry instead of partitions, one
generous space the plan is organized around. Satisfy the brief and the
rules above first; then spend your remaining freedom on character, and
be able to say in one sentence what the idea is.

## Before you emit

Walk the plan as a resident: enter, find the light switch-side of each
door, reach every room, host a guest, carry groceries to the kitchen.
Then check: all rooms reachable · bath off circulation · no room under
minimum · aspects sane · doors ≥ minimums, clear of junctions · every
habitable room windowed · labels everywhere · dims outside.
